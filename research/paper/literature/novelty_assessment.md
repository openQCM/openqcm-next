# Novelty assessment: openQCM NEXT impedance-analysis method (v2, 2026-10-08)

Reference numbers refer to `literature_review.md`; verification grades (V/I/U) as defined there. This version supersedes the 2026-10-06 assessment after a verification pass in which the full texts of the closest prior works (Sakti 2019, Horst 2022, Muñoz 2023/2026, Burda 2022a,b, Leppin 2020, Johannsmann 2021, Ciarrocchi 2026, the Q-1 v3.0 host source, the rheoQCM source, the AWSensors patent, the Gao/Khalil/Probst microwave papers) were read. "Not found" still means "not found by web search, Europe PMC, Crossref and OpenAlex on 2026-10-08", not a Scopus/Web of Science sweep.

## (i) What is prior art

1. **The phase-shifted Lorentzian (PSL).** Johannsmann's group writes the resonance as Y(f) = e^{iφ}·iΓG_max/(f_res − f + iΓ) + G_off + iB_off, six real parameters, and fits G and B: Leppin et al. 2020, eq. 3 [35] (V); Johannsmann, Langhoff, Leppin 2021, eq. 13 [5] (V); the 2015 monograph already lists G_max, G_off, B_off and φ as fit parameters of its eq. 4.5.30 [36] (V for the chapter glossary; section not read); the 2024 primer defines it in its glossary [7] (I). Neither 2020 nor 2021 cites an origin for the function; use before 2014 could not be confirmed or excluded [37,38,114,115,116] (U). Independently of Johannsmann, the Arnau/AWSensors group patented a six-parameter Lorentzian with "an angle that takes into account the small slopes" fitted simultaneously to G and B (priority July 2020) [112] (V), and the Shull group's open-source rheoQCM has fitted G and B jointly with a phase-shifted Lorentzian since at least 2019 [107] (V, code). The manuscript must present the PSL as an established fit function.
2. **The rotation angle as a calibration/background artefact.** Johannsmann: φ "accounts for an asymmetry of the resonance curve. Imperfect calibration causes such an asymmetry" [5] (V). In microwave resonator metrology the same factor e^{iφ} inside the line shape is Gao's φ_0 (2008) [117], interpreted as impedance mismatch by Khalil et al. 2012 [40] and implemented algebraically by Probst et al. 2015 [41]; a real "skew" term on |S21| is in Petersan and Anlage 1998 [33] (all V). The G-only PSL is the single-quadrature special case of the rotated circle fit.
3. **Conductance maximum + half-height width as the standard estimator**, and its known bias with noise and coarse grids: Schröder et al. 2001 [26], the QCM-I vendor note [27], Arnau's 2008 review (recommends a plain Lorentzian fit for heavy loading) [16], Robinson and Clegg 2005 [94], Petersan and Anlage 1998 [33], Niedermayer et al. 2012 [34], Kang et al. 2007 [95], Na Songkhla and Nakamoto 2018 [96] (V).
4. **Complex impedance in polar form from an AD8302 through a known resistor.** Textbook in bioimpedance and EIS: Yang et al. 2006 [45], Mohamadou et al. 2018 [46], Ciarrocchi et al. 2026 [47], Ben Atitallah et al. 2022 [74], Nogales et al. 2026 [75] (V); the last uses the same 52.3 Ω input matching as the openQCM board. None treats a resonator; none resolves the 0–180° fold (Ciarrocchi: "largest inaccuracies near 0° and ±180°"; Nogales: unexplained "±") (V).
5. **AD8302 phase-sign ambiguity and its resolution in hardware**: switched in-phase/quadrature reference (McDermott and Ireland 2004 [71]), −90° reference shift (Wetterlin 2007 [72]), second AD8302 at 90° (Mlynek et al. 2017 [70]; Grossmann et al. 2016 [69]) (V). Hobby VNA software infers the sign of X from the trend of |Z| (miniVNA/Zplots, grey, I).
6. **AD8302 with resonators**: Sakti et al. 2019, QCM in air, AD9910 DDS, 50 Ω series resistor, |Z| and phase plotted separately, no G + jB, no f/Γ estimate [49] (V); Love-wave and SAW sensors with DDS + AD8302 at fixed frequency in liquid [64,65]; LC, phononic, RF-MEMS and magnetoelastic resonators [66,67,68,50] (V). None reconstructs Y or resolves the fold.
7. **The hardware.** AD9851 DDS, quartz in series with a 52.3 Ω resistor (the usual AD8302 input-termination value, also used in [75]; I), AD8302, Teensy 4.0 is the published openQCM Q-1/NEXT [51,52]; described in a peer-reviewed review from vendor material [62] (V); reused with the AD8302 replaced by an AD8310 log amplifier by Horst et al. 2022 [53] (V). The published Q-1 v3.0 host software works on the raw AD8302 magnitude only: baseline polynomial, Savitzky–Golay, spline, argmax, width at 0.707 × peak, D = 1/Q; the phase trace is acquired and plotted but not used; no divider inversion, no Lorentzian (V, source read). The NEXT uses a per-overtone calibrated cut-off instead of 0.707 [110] (V, vendor post).
8. **Multi-overtone Kanazawa–Gordon validations by impedance analysis**, all on bench or lab-built hardware: Yoshimoto et al. 2004 (√N, I), Daridon et al. 2011 (up to eight overtones; 5 % from f, 2 % from bandwidth; I), Cassiède et al. 2011 (fundamental deviates from the higher-overtone trend; I), McHale et al. 2008 (3rd harmonic slope ≈ 10 % below KG; V), Bund and Schwitzgebel 1998 (V), Kankare et al. 2006 (V), Leppin et al. 2020 (exponent ≈ 1/2, ΔΓ = −Δf; V), Johannsmann 2021 (−Δf = ΔΓ = 716 Hz/√n for water at 5 MHz; V) [8,80,81,83,84,79,35,5].
9. **Low-cost / open-source QCM-D 2020–2026 with liquid data**: Horst 2022 (water, qualitative KG) [53]; Muñoz 2023/2026 (VNWA / NanoVNA-H, PEG, KG cited only) [54,55]; Fort 2021/2024 and Addabbo 2022 (glucose, BVD-Newtonian model, no BOM) [13,23,101]; Burda 2022 (AD2, glycerol, KG cited only) [30,31]; Adel 2024 (oscillator, glycerol, no KG) [104]; Kırımlı group (antenna analysers / Keysight E4990A, glycerol, KG as ML baseline) [61,106]; Miranda-Martínez 2021 (openQCM Q-1 vs viscometer) [63] (all V). No low-cost instrument paper reports numeric per-overtone deviations from KG over a liquid series (not found).

## (ii) What combination is defensibly new

Against the items above, the elements for which no prior QCM publication was found are:

1. **Exact analytical inversion of the series divider from the two AD8302 outputs to Z_q and Y = G + jB on a DDS/gain-phase-detector QCM.** Burda inverts a reference-resistor divider but with two sampled channels (vector) [30,31]; Sakti has the same chip and divider but stops at |Z| and phase [49]; bioimpedance work inverts the AD8302 but never on a resonator [45,46,47,74,75].
2. **Software-only resolution of the AD8302 phase fold using the resonance** (the reactance changes sign through series resonance), in place of a hardware quadrature reference [69,70,71,72]. The closest analogue is trend-based sign inference in hobby VNA software (grey, I) and must be acknowledged.
3. **Application of the (prior) phase-shifted Lorentzian to G(f) obtained this way**, with a side-by-side comparison against argmax + half-height on the same sweeps, and with the fitted φ interpreted as the residual phase of the AD8302/divider chain (board phase) — a specific instance of the "imperfect calibration" reading of φ [5] and of the "impedance mismatch" reading in microwave work [40,117].
4. **Numeric multi-overtone Kanazawa–Gordon validation (n = 1–9) in water, isopropanol and glucose solutions on two units of a sub-€5 k instrument**, with the √n collapse of Δf and ΔΓ and the ΔΓ/(−Δf) ratio, reported with error definitions aligned to [13,80,81]. The published openQCM software and all openQCM-derived papers lack this [51,52,53,54,55,63].
5. **Release of the analysis as open source on the published open hardware**, which is what HardwareX/Hardware/Sensors reviewers weigh.

Items 1–2 are technical; 3–5 are methodological/validation contributions on known elements. None of them is a new physical result.

## (iii) Novelty statement

The instrument is the published openQCM NEXT (AD9851 DDS, quartz in series with a 52.3 Ω resistor, AD8302 gain/phase detector, Teensy 4.0); no hardware is added or modified. The contribution is a signal-processing mode for this hardware: the two scalar detector outputs are inverted exactly through the divider to the complex admittance Y = G + jB at every sweep point, and the sign of the unsigned AD8302 phase is recovered from the resonance itself rather than from an added quadrature reference. On the resulting G(f), resonance frequency and half-bandwidth are obtained both with the conductance-maximum/half-height estimator and with the phase-shifted Lorentzian of Johannsmann and co-workers, which is prior art and is used without modification; the two estimators are compared on the same sweeps, and the fitted phase is interpreted as the residual phase of the detector chain. The result is validated against the Kanazawa–Gordon relation in water, isopropanol and glucose solutions on overtones 1–9 and on two instruments, which no published DDS/gain-phase-detector QCM, including the openQCM host software itself, has reported.
